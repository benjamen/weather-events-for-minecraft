# Weather Events: design

Six channels, each a level from 0 to 3 (none, light, medium, heavy), that combine: rain, wind, hail, snow, sun and mist.
Rain with wind is a gale; snow with wind is a blizzard; sun with a breeze is a scorcher.

## What is built
- `channels.js` (pure, tested): which combinations are possible. Hail needs a storm, snow only in the cold, blistering
  sun means a dry sky, and the hot biomes make the sun worse. `describeWeather` names the result.
- `forecast.js` (pure, tested): seeded "fronts" (spells of weather lasting a few minutes) that follow believable
  sequences, with an `outlook` for a barometer item.
- `puddles.js` (pure, tested): where puddles form, how fast, how deep (up to a quarter block), how long they last, and
  how sun and wind dry them.
- `wind.js` (pure, tested): a wind that drifts and gusts, the shove on a player (braced by crouching, stopped by
  shelter, worse in the air), and the leaf and howl numbers.
- `weather.js` (Minecraft glue): runs the forecast, eases the channels, drives vanilla rain and thunder, asks the
  shared fog mixer for the haze of each kind of weather, and pushes players in strong wind. Announces
  `wx:changed` so other packs and games can react.

- `puddle-blocks.js` + the `wx:puddle` block (Minecraft glue and assets): a custom block with a `wx:size` state of 1 to 4
  sixteenths deep (a quarter block at most), a translucent water-blob texture and one thin-slab model per depth. In
  rain, puddles form on open ground near each player, grow to the rain's limit, then shrink and dry, faster in sun and
  wind. Walking through one splashes. The list is saved in a world property so a restart never strands puddles.
  `scripts/gen-puddle.mjs` generates the block, models and texture from `MAX_SIZE`, so the art and the rules agree.
  `/scriptevent wx:puddles` counts them and `/scriptevent wx:dry` clears them all.
- `hail.js` (pure, tested) and `hail-stones.js` (glue): stones are vanilla snowballs tagged `wx_hail`, fired from open sky above
  each player. Level 1 pea stones are harmless and just patter on roofs (sound follows the block: wood, stone, glass,
  metal); level 2 marbles sting; level 3 golf balls hurt and can break glass. A helmet takes most of the damage.
  Switch off with world properties `wx:hail_damage` / `wx:hail_glass` set to `off`. Untested in a game.
- `streams.js` (pure, tested) and `wind-streams.js` (glue): `wx:leaf` particles (`RP/particles/wx_leaf.json`) spawn upwind
  of each player and drift downwind, faster in gusts, plus a `wx.wind` howl that comes more often in a gale. The leaf
  texture and howl are generated by `scripts/gen-wind-assets.py` (original, CC0; see `assets/audio/LICENSES.md`).
  Untested in a game: check the particle shows and drifts the right way.
- `sun.js` / `sun-effects.js`: from sun 2 (hot) an exposed player without a helmet gets hungry, at 3 (blistering) slow and sunburnt
  (1 damage every ~8 to 30 s). Shade, roofs, water and helmets protect. Heat haze comes through the fog mixer.
- `snow.js` / `snow-layers.js`: snow piles base-game snow layers on open ground near players (up to 1, 3 or 6 layers by level),
  saved in a world property and melting layer by layer after the snow stops (faster in sun and warmth). `wx:melt` clears them.
  Untested in a game: check the `height` state is right for `minecraft:snow_layer`.
- `barometer.js` / `barometer-item.js`: a craftable `wx:barometer` (copper and redstone). Use it to see the weather, what is next and
  when, and whether pressure is rising or falling.
- `wx:flake` falling-snow particles (`flakes` in `wind-streams.js`), carried by the wind, thicker in heavier snow.
- `mist` channel: haze through the fog mixer (visibility about 70, 28 or 9 blocks), only in still air, burnt off by a strong sun,
  gone in hail, and thick mist hides the sun. `/scriptevent wx:set mist 3` or `wx:front fog`.

## Honest limits
- Vanilla rain is only on or off. Light rain is faked with fog and (later) particles.
- Vanilla trees cannot bend. The plan is leaf and dust streams, plus animated flags, windsocks and banners.
- Sun brightness is a glare overlay and haze, not real brightness.
- Thunder: the base game plays it the instant lightning strikes, and a pack cannot delay or remove that, so there is no
  distance delay between flash and thunder.

## Not built yet
- Hail particles (rain uses the base game's).
- Sun goggles and hat items (a helmet of any kind stands in for now). A true screen glare overlay (heat haze is fog).
 Wetness, mud, rainbows.
- Biome temperature: the Script API cannot read a biome's climate easily, so for now `wx:climate cold|temperate|hot`
  sets it for the world.

## To check first in a running game
- **The puddle block first:** `/scriptevent wx:set rain 3`, wait, and look at the ground. If puddles do not appear, check
  the content log for the block failing to load. The block file uses format version 1.21.80 with a collision box
  half a block tall at most; if the game rejects the box or the permutations, that is the file to fix. Puddles should
  look like thin round water patches, you should walk over them without stepping up, and they should dry after
  `wx:set rain 0` (try `wx:set sun 3` to see it faster).
Install both packs in a world with cheats on, then:
- `/scriptevent wx:set rain 2 wind 3`: rain should start within about 20 seconds, fog should close in a little, and
  you should be shoved downwind when you stand in the open. Crouch to brace; stand under a roof to be left alone.
- `/scriptevent wx:front storm`, `wx:front blizzard`, `wx:front scorcher`, then `wx:auto` to follow the forecast.
- `/scriptevent wx:status` prints the weather name, the forecast and how long until the next front.
- `/scriptevent wx:climate cold` then `wx:set rain 3`: it should snow instead.
- Whether `applyKnockback` moves a player at these strengths, and whether the push feels like wind or like being hit.
- Whether the shared fog mixer pushes one fog per player without flicker.
